## Purpose

This workbook helps organizations establish and operate an AI red team, define governance and authorization requirements, plan repeatable test campaigns, document evidence, prioritize findings, and evaluate the security of AI systems and agentic AI environments.

It is designed to support both model-focused evaluation and broader implementation testing across applications, APIs, RAG pipelines, agents, tools, identities, infrastructure, data flows, and operational controls.

## Main Capabilities

- Define the AI red-team mission, authority, independence, roles, and operating model.
- Establish rules of engagement, scope, prohibited actions, stop conditions, evidence handling, and escalation paths.
- Build a multidisciplinary team covering AI security, application security, cloud, infrastructure, data, privacy, safety, threat intelligence, and incident response.
- Assess governance, models, prompts, RAG, agents, tools, memory, identities, permissions, supply chain, deployment, monitoring, and recovery controls.
- Plan test campaigns with objectives, hypotheses, scenarios, methods, owners, evidence, status, and retesting requirements.
- Track findings, severity, remediation ownership, risk acceptance, and validation results.
- Cross-reference red-team activities with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE.
- Maintain an APA 7 source register for relevant AI security and red-team guidance.

## Recommended Use

1. Define the system, business context, assets, users, data, models, agents, tools, and deployment environments in scope.
2. Establish authority, rules of engagement, safety constraints, evidence requirements, and escalation paths.
3. Select relevant checklist controls and assign owners and status.
4. Build a threat-informed test campaign covering realistic misuse and attack scenarios.
5. Capture reproducible evidence, expected versus observed behavior, and affected components.
6. Prioritize findings using organizational risk tolerance and impact criteria.
7. Retest mitigations and track residual risk, accepted exceptions, and lessons learned.

## Important Note

The workbook is a planning and assurance aid. Red-team activities must be explicitly authorized and conducted in controlled conditions that protect people, data, systems, customers, and business operations.
